Dune Dash: Privacy Policy

Effective date: 3 November 2026
Developer: sebaworldgames
Contact: sebaworldgames321@gmail.com. Write to this address with any privacy question or request.

This policy covers the Dune Dash app for Android (package com.sebaworldgames.dunedash), including its Kids mode and Pro mode. You can also read it inside the game: Settings > About > Privacy policy.

The short version

We (the developer) never receive any information about you or your child, and we don't sell any. The game itself sends nothing: the app doesn't request Android's internet permission. Only four things can ever leave the app, and each one only if you choose it: a picture you Share; a friend card, when you send it and on challenge links you send while signed in to Google Play Games, unless you turn that off in Settings (Pro mode, see Friend cards below); your Play Games details if you sign in to Google Play Games in Pro mode (Google's Play Games library in the app then sends your Play Games name and ID, your Pro scores with the dinosaur you ran with, and Google's own usage and diagnostics data to Google); and your phone's own backup, if you have turned that on. All four are explained below. It has no ads, no analytics of our own, no accounts of our own and no in-app purchases.

What the app stores on your device

The game saves the following on your device, inside the app's private storage (and in your phone's own backup, if you have turned that on; see Device backup below):

  • Game progress: best scores and distances, gems, unlocked characters and colours, missions, badges, level and XP, medals, Daily run results, eggs, and the dinosaurs in your Nursery.
  • Names you give your baby dinosaurs (up to 12 letters). These names are stored on the device and are never sent to us or to anyone else.
  • Your settings, such as sound, music, vibration, brightness, motion effects and whether reminders are on.
  • If you turn reminders on: the time and text of each upcoming reminder, so it can be shown on time and restored after the phone restarts.
  • When you use Share: one temporary picture of your run in the app's cache. A new share replaces it.
  • Challenge codes (Pro mode): up to five you played and up to five you opened but haven't played yet (those are removed after 7 days). For each: the code, level, distance, when you got it and, if the link carried a friend card, the sender's Play Games name.
  • Which mode you picked when you first opened the game.
  • Friend cards you add (Pro mode, 13 and over): up to 50 friends, each with the name, Play Games player ID, dinosaur and best scores from their card. Remove a friend with ✕ in Friends; Reset progress removes them all.
  • Google Play Games (Pro mode): whether it is on, whether the game has already asked you about it, whether your Play Games friends list may be used, and the answer to the age question, kept only as "may be under 13" or "13 and over" (the birth year itself is not kept).

We (the developer) cannot see or access any of this information.

Permissions the app uses

  • Vibration: short vibrations during play. You can turn this off in Settings.
  • Notifications (Android 13 and newer): asked for only if you choose to turn reminders on. The game offers this once when you first play, and you can change it at any time in Settings. Reminders tell you when an egg is ready to hatch and when a new Daily run is ready. They are created on the device; nothing is sent from a server.
  • Run at startup (RECEIVE_BOOT_COMPLETED): puts your reminders back after the phone restarts or the app updates.

Sharing

At the end of any run you can tap Share. The app makes a picture showing the run's scene, distance, score, gems, dinosaur, world, mode and date (and medals for a Daily run), with a short line of text. The text also has a link to Dune Dash on Google Play. It hands the picture to the Android share menu, and it goes only to the app you choose (for example a messaging app). After that, that app's own privacy policy applies. Nothing is shared unless you tap Share and pick an app. The picture contains no names, account details or location.

After a challenge run in Pro mode, the picture and text also carry a 10-character challenge code, and the text carries a link with that code in it (sebaworldgames321-droid.github.io/c/?code). The code holds only the course, the level and speed setting, the distance and which dinosaur was used. It holds nothing about you. While you're signed in to Google Play Games, the link also carries your friend card (the game-over screen says so, and Settings can turn it off); see Friend cards below. If the person you send it to has Dune Dash, tapping the link opens the game straight into that challenge; typing, pasting or opening a code works on the phone alone and nothing is looked up online. Paste code reads your clipboard only when you tap it, and only a challenge code found there is used. If they don't have the game, the link opens a small page on GitHub Pages that shows the code and a link to Google Play; GitHub's privacy statement applies to that visit, and we don't receive anything from it.

Google Play Games: leaderboards and friends (Pro mode, optional)

Kids mode never uses Google Play Games. The first time you open Pro mode in the Android app, after its short tour, the game asks for your birth year. If you may be under 13, Google Play Games stays off on that phone and nothing is sent. If you are 13 or over, Google Play Games signs you in, or Google asks you to sign in or create a Play Games profile, and you can cancel; you can also say Not now to the age question and turn it on later in Settings.

Signing in to Google Play Games, a Google service, links the game to your Play Games profile (your Play Games name and player ID). While it is on, the game signs in by itself each time it starts. If Google doesn't sign you in on its own, the game opens Google's sign-in screen once, on the menu; if you cancel, it won't open it by itself again until you sign in yourself. Play Games never starts in Kids mode. At the end of each Pro run the app gives Google Play Games your score for that level's leaderboard, with a short tag saying which dinosaur you ran with so the leaderboards can show it (the Daily and Fast boards take Normal and Hard runs, and the Daily board uses distance; challenge runs are not sent). Google Play Games holds your profile and those scores and shows them to other players according to your Play Games profile settings. Google's Play Games library also collects its own usage and diagnostics data. Google may ask whether the game can see your gaming community list (Play Games players you follow who follow you back), which the game calls friends. If you agree, the game uses your friends' names and scores to show the Friends view, where you rank among friends after a run, and which friend to chase next; they are shown on screen and not stored. Tapping a player on a leaderboard opens Google's own Play Games profile screen for that player. Following other players happens in Google Play, and Google handles it. Google's privacy policy (policies.google.com/privacy) applies to that data. To show a leaderboard, the app asks Google Play Games for the names and scores on it; they are shown on screen and not stored. We (the developer) don't receive any of this.

Friend cards (Pro mode, 13 and over). When you're signed in to Google Play Games you can tap Send my card in Friends; the first time, the game shows what your card contains. Your challenge links carry it too, unless you turn off Friend card in challenge links in Settings. A friend card holds your Play Games name and player ID, the dinosaur you have selected, your best Pro scores, today's Daily distances and when the card was made; anyone who gets the link can see them. You choose who gets it on your phone's share screen. The card sits after the # in the link, so opening the link sends it to no website (not even ours). When you add a friend's card, the game keeps it on this phone only, to show that friend in Friends, on your Friends boards and, while you're signed in, after your runs. A newer card from the same friend updates their scores. Remove a friend with ✕ in Friends; uninstalling the game, clearing its data or Reset progress removes them all. In Kids mode, a friend card link only offers to switch to Pro mode after a grown-up check. Friend cards are never sent to us.

You can turn Google Play Games off at any time in Settings; from that moment the game sends and loads nothing more from it. Scores already sent stay on Google Play Games until you remove them there, from your Play Games profile settings.

Children

Dune Dash includes a Kids mode for young children. Because the app collects no personal information from anyone, it collects none from children. Kids mode has no leaderboards, no sign-in, no challenge codes, no chat and no way to contact other players (a challenge link opened in Kids mode only offers to switch to Pro mode, after a grown-up check), and the whole app has no ads and no purchases. In Pro mode, the age question keeps Google Play Games (leaderboards and friends) switched off for anyone under 13. The first time the game is opened on a phone, it asks who is playing (Kids or Pro mode); in Pro mode the age question comes before anything uses Google Play Games.

Device backup

Android may include the app's saved data in your device backup to your own Google account, if you have turned on backup in your phone's settings. Google handles this backup under your Google account settings. We cannot access it.

Keeping and deleting your data

Your data stays on your device (and in your own phone backup, if that's on) until you delete it:

  • Settings > Data > Reset progress in the game erases all scores, gems, characters, eggs, missions, challenge codes and settings (except the answer to the leaderboard age question), and cancels any scheduled reminders. Scores already sent to Google Play Games are removed there, not in the game.
  • To remove everything, including any scheduled reminders, uninstall the app or use Android Settings > Apps > Dune Dash > Storage > Clear storage.

Because we never receive your data, we have nothing stored about you that we could delete or hand over.

Changes to this policy

If the app changes how it handles information, we will update this policy and the effective date above before the change is released.
